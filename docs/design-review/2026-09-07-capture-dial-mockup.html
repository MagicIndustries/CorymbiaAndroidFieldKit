The ring is the clock. The filled circle is the accuracy itself — a real radius, shrinking onto the crosshair as the fix converges. Driven by the series measured on the S25: 7.5 m down to 1.4 m at one reading a second.

What to judge, at arm's length

  • The lock: the crosshair lights, one flare goes out, and the label reads LOCKED ON. Is it unmistakable at a glance — and is it too much?
  • Do the ring and the circle read as one thing converging, or two things racing?
  • Switch to Poor: the circle stops short and never locks. Is that legible as “this one never settled”?
  • Anything drawing the eye before the accuracy and the seconds is too loud.

The crosshair is sized to 1.4 m — the sharpest the S25 reached outdoors on 7 September, clear sky. So the radius always means metres, and a good fix lands exactly on the target while a worse one stops outside it.

Retracted. Spec §9.2.1 corrected that sentence: the crosshair is at 1.4 m while anything under 5 m grades as good, so a green circle resting well outside the crosshair is what a normal capture looks like — which is why the settled and locked completion levels exist. A fix that reaches the hardware floor lands on the crosshair; one that stops short draws outside it.
